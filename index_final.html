About this project

A Tuesday at Cornell is an interactive fictional account of a college student preparing for a group presentation and fundraiser. The reader makes predictions about everyday situations and then sees how social psychology can help explain them. The scenes build on one another so that Alex’s misunderstandings, group decisions, and plans have consequences later in the day.

The project draws on course material about egocentrism and the spotlight effect, overconfidence, construal and framing, norms, situationism, evolutionary accounts of cooperation, moral foundations, questionable beliefs, social facilitation, emotion, conformity, terror management, and cognitive dissonance. Each scene identifies its main concepts and a relevant lecture or study. The examples illustrate possible mechanisms; they do not prove that any single psychological process caused a fictional person’s behavior.
